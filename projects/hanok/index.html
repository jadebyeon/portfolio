HANOK

Reimagining Korean Cultural Heritage Through Contemporary Storytelling

Hanok is a cultural design project that blends traditional Korean narratives with modern aesthetics to engage global audiences. I led the storytelling, illustration direction, and product design for a new brand that reinterprets Korean heritage through contemporary visual language. The project began with a modern retelling of the Korean folktale Princess Bari and expanded to include illustrated merchandise concepts inspired by Korean art, architecture, and symbolism.

Academic

Design Problem

While Korean pop culture has become globally popular, deeper cultural stories and visual traditions remain underrepresented or stereotyped. Traditional stories like Princess Bari carry powerful messages, but are often inaccessible to non-Korean audiences due to outdated formats or lack of engaging design. The challenge was to reframe this content with care—avoiding appropriation—while introducing a visual and narrative style that feels both fresh and rooted in heritage.

Research & Strategy

I began with cultural and visual research on Princess Bari, including variations of the folktale, historical motifs, and symbolism in Korean textiles, temple paintings, and folk art. Key strategies included:

  • Mapping narrative arcs of Princess Bari to emotional journeys relevant to modern readers
  • Studying how brands like Ghibli and Disney localize folklore while maintaining authenticity
  • Experimenting with illustration styles that depart from Disney mimicry toward bolder, contemporary editorial aesthetics
  • Identifying iconographic elements (e.g., the lotus, water, ritual) for integration into visual motifs and product design

Visual System Development

  • Shifted illustration style from traditional cartoon mimicry to a more expressive, painterly digital style to evoke emotion and cultural nuance
  • Created illustrated spreads and branding collateral that highlight themes of loss, transformation, and resilience within the narrative

Cultural Product Design

  • Designed mockups of merchandise (e.g., art prints, packaging, stickers) incorporating Korean patterns and material aesthetics
  • Used Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop to produce digital prototypes, with the goal of translating a few pieces into physical prototypes
  • Proposed modular product sets for gallery retail, pop-ups, or cultural exhibitions

Deliverables

Reflection

This project deepened my understanding of how to translate intangible cultural heritage into visual storytelling that feels both personal and sharable. I learned the importance of resisting surface-level representation, instead crafting experiences that reflect values, philosophy, and emotional truth. In future iterations, I’d like to include co-creation with Korean diaspora artists or historians to further enrich narrative accuracy and representation.

Hanok taught me that cultural storytelling through design requires both reverence and reinvention, and that visual systems can become bridges between worlds.
